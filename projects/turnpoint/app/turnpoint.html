How Turnpoint works

Six published cycle estimators read each chart: Ehlers' roofing filter with a Fourier spectrum, a detrended FFT, and four estimators ported from lastguru's open DominantCycle library (MESA/MAMA, Pearson autocorrelation, DFT, phase accumulation). Each finds the dominant cycle length and where today sits in it. Turnpoint averages those positions around the cycle, applies the median cycle length, and projects the next trough and crest, which always fall half a cycle apart.

Around the dates sit two context modules. The trend trail (an ATR trailing stop) shows the prevailing direction; a close through it marks a trend change. The volatility module places price in sigma zones around its 20-bar mean and draws a cone of where price would land 68% and 95% of the time if it moved randomly.

Every forecast is written to an append-only ledger before its date arrives and scored afterwards. A date counts as a hit when a real turn, a close that is the lowest or highest within five bars either side, lands within two bars of it. The same rule is applied to randomly timed dates, so every hit rate here sits next to what luck would score.

Educational analysis, not financial advice. Cycle projections are estimates; markets do not follow cycles reliably.
